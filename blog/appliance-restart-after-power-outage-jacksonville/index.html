After a Power Outage: A Safe Appliance Restart Checklist for Jacksonville Homes

A practical, safety-first checklist for restarting refrigerators, washers, dryers, dishwashers and ovens after a Jacksonville power outage.

By Mason Hedlund · Published OCT 17, 2026 · Jacksonville, FL

Jacksonville storms can turn the power off for a few seconds, a few hours or several times in one afternoon. When electricity returns, the safest move is not to start pressing every button in the house. Give the supply a moment to stabilize, check each appliance deliberately and write down what changed.

I am Mason Hedlund with Hedlund Appliance Service. This is the order I recommend for homeowners in Jacksonville, Jacksonville Beach, Atlantic Beach, Neptune Beach, Ponte Vedra Beach and nearby neighborhoods after an outage or repeated power flicker.

Start with the house, not the appliance

If lights are still dimming, power is cycling on and off, you smell hot wiring or you see damage at an outlet, leave the appliance off and contact the utility or a licensed electrician. An appliance cannot operate correctly on an unstable supply, and repeated restart attempts can make diagnosis harder.

Do not remove outlet covers, open an electrical panel or bypass a breaker. If an ordinary household breaker is visibly tripped and the home has stable power, reset it only if you are comfortable using the panel normally. A breaker that trips again is a stop sign, not an invitation to keep resetting it.

Refrigerator and freezer: protect the food first

Keep the doors closed while power is out. After power returns, confirm the interior light and control panel come back, then listen for normal fan and compressor activity. Many refrigerators intentionally delay compressor startup for several minutes, so silence immediately after restoration does not prove a failure.

Use appliance thermometers rather than guessing by touch. The refrigerator should move toward about 37°F and the freezer toward 0°F. Cooling can take hours after a long outage, especially when doors were opened or the kitchen is warm. If the freezer becomes cold but the fresh-food side stays warm, review my guide to refrigerator airflow problems.

Food-safety note: When you are unsure how long food was above a safe temperature, follow current USDA guidance. Appliance repair can restore cooling, but it cannot determine whether stored food is safe to eat.

Washer: check for an interrupted cycle

A washer may return with the door locked, water in the tub or a paused cycle. Give the control a few minutes, then use the manufacturer's normal cancel or drain command. Do not force a locked door. Front-load machines often keep the lock engaged while they detect water or wait for a safe condition.

If the washer hums without draining, displays an error or repeatedly restarts the same step, stop the cycle and note the code. The timing matters: did it fail during fill, wash, drain or spin? My washer drain guide explains safe observations that help narrow the problem.

Dryer: confirm airflow before running a full load

Check that the display powers up and the drum turns on a short test. If the dryer uses gas, do not move it or touch the gas connection. If you smell gas, leave the area and contact the gas utility or emergency services from a safe location.

For an electric dryer, a working display does not prove the heating circuit has full power. If the drum turns but there is no heat after an outage, stop rather than repeatedly running long cycles. If the breaker trips, the cabinet becomes unusually hot or you smell burning, arrange service. Review the dryer breaker safety guide for more context.

Dishwasher: look for standing water and a remembered cycle

Some dishwashers resume automatically; others cancel the cycle. Open the door carefully and check for standing water, loose items and detergent that never released. Use the normal cancel-drain command once. Do not pour drain cleaner into the tub and do not reach into the sump while the machine is connected to power.

If the dishwasher will not drain, confirm the sink and disposal are not backed up. A power outage may simply reveal a drainage restriction that was already developing. See the guide to a dishwasher that will not drain.

Oven and range: reset the clock before judging operation

Many ovens will not bake until the clock is set after an outage. Clear any flashing time display and follow the owner's manual for a basic reset. Do not test a gas oven repeatedly if ignition is delayed, gas odor is present or clicking continues without a flame.

An error code that returns after the clock is set can reflect a sensor, control or power problem. Photograph the code before clearing it. If an electric oven has a working display but no heat, remember that the controls and heating elements can use different parts of the electrical supply.

One clean restart is enough

If the owner's manual recommends unplugging an appliance, disconnect it only when the plug is safely accessible and your hands and floor are dry. Wait several minutes, reconnect it and observe one normal test. Avoid a loop of unplugging, resetting and starting over; repeated attempts can erase useful symptoms without fixing the cause.

Before scheduling service, gather the model number, any error code, the outage timing and a short description of what still works. My checklist on what to note before calling appliance repair can save time.

When to arrange appliance service

Call for help when a breaker trips again, a control stays dead after stable power is confirmed, an error returns, cooling does not trend in the right direction, water remains trapped or the appliance smells hot. FAQs

Should I unplug every appliance before a storm?

Small electronics can often be unplugged safely in advance, but large appliances may have hard-to-reach plugs, water lines or gas connections. Do not pull out a heavy appliance or disturb a gas line just to unplug it.

How long should a refrigerator take to restart?

Many models use a protective delay of several minutes. Temperature recovery after a long outage can take several hours. Watch the temperature trend instead of expecting instant cooling.

Can a brief power flicker damage an appliance control?

It can, but a changed display or temporary error does not automatically mean the board failed. Stable power, the model's reset procedure and a proper diagnosis should come before parts are ordered.
