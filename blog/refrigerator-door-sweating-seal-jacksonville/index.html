Why Refrigerator Doors Sweat in Florida's Humidity

Condensation around a refrigerator door is often an air-leak or humidity clue. Mason Hedlund explains safe checks for Jacksonville homeowners.

By Mason Hedlund · Published OCT 12, 2026 · Jacksonville, FL

A refrigerator that is cold inside but sweating around the door can make you think the thermostat is failing. In Jacksonville, I usually start somewhere simpler: the door seal, the way the door is closing, and the amount of warm humid air getting into the cabinet.

I am Mason Hedlund with Hedlund Appliance Service. This problem comes up around Jacksonville Beach, Atlantic Beach, Neptune Beach, Ponte Vedra Beach and inland neighborhoods too. Florida humidity makes a small sealing problem show itself quickly. The moisture is a symptom; the useful question is where the warm air is getting in.

First, notice where the moisture is forming

Condensation only along one corner of the gasket points in a different direction than water across the entire cabinet. A wet spot near the top may come from a door that is slightly out of alignment. Moisture between French doors may involve the center flap, sometimes called the mullion. Water under the refrigerator could be a drain or supply-line problem instead of a door-seal issue.

Wipe the area dry, close the door normally and check again after an hour or two. That simple observation tells me more than turning the temperature colder and hoping for the best.

Safe checks you can do without taking anything apart

  • Look for food packages, bins or shelves keeping the door from closing fully.
  • Clean sticky residue from the gasket with mild soap and water, then dry it.
  • Check the gasket for a folded corner, split, hardened section or visible gap.
  • Make sure the refrigerator is stable and the door is not drifting open on its own.
  • On a French-door model, confirm the center flap moves into position when the door closes.

A paper test can help: close the door on a plain sheet of paper and gently pull. You should feel resistance. Test a few spots around the door. This is not a perfect diagnosis, but one loose section can reveal a gasket or alignment problem.

Jacksonville humidity matters: humid air carries moisture. Every time it slips through a weak seal, that moisture can collect on the cabinet, gasket or food compartments.

Why turning the refrigerator colder can backfire

Lowering the setting does not repair an air leak. It can make the refrigerator run longer, build extra frost and sometimes freeze food near vents. If the fresh-food section is already too cold, see my guide on a refrigerator freezing food. If cooling is weak throughout the cabinet, start with my Jacksonville refrigerator cooling checklist.

When the gasket is not the whole problem

A good-looking gasket can still leak if the door is sagging, a hinge is worn, the cabinet is not level or an inner door panel is distorted. Some models also use an anti-sweat heater around the door area. If the moisture is widespread and the seal checks out, the diagnosis may need to go beyond the gasket.

Do not try to bend hinges aggressively or remove powered door components. Modern refrigerator doors can contain wiring, water tubing and controls. A small alignment mistake can turn a manageable repair into a larger one.

When to schedule refrigerator repair

Call when condensation keeps returning, frost builds near the door, food temperatures rise, the door will not stay shut or you see damage around the hinge. FAQs

Does condensation mean my refrigerator is failing?

Not necessarily. A dirty, folded or damaged gasket is common, especially when the refrigerator still holds temperature. Persistent moisture still deserves attention because warm air makes the unit work harder.

Can I replace a refrigerator gasket myself?

Some gaskets are straightforward and others require careful fitting or door disassembly. Confirm the model number and the actual cause before ordering a part.

Why is there moisture between French doors?

The center flap may not be closing correctly, the doors may be misaligned or the cabinet's anti-condensation system may not be operating as designed.
