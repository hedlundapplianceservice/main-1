A Cleaner Dishwasher Between Cycles: Jacksonville Maintenance That Prevents Odors

A practical dishwasher maintenance routine for reducing odors, residue and standing-water smells in humid Jacksonville kitchens.

By Mason Hedlund · Published OCT 18, 2026 · Jacksonville, FL

A dishwasher should not make the kitchen smell stale between loads. In Jacksonville, warm rooms and humid air can make a small amount of trapped food residue noticeable faster, but humidity is usually an amplifier—not the original cause. The useful question is where moisture and debris are being left behind.

I am Mason Hedlund with Hedlund Appliance Service. When a dishwasher odor keeps returning, I look at the filter, sump area, door seals, drain path and everyday loading habits before assuming the machine needs a major part.

Start with the type and timing of the odor

A sour or musty smell after the door stays closed usually points toward damp residue. A sewage-like odor can come from the sink drain, disposal or drain-hose installation. A hot electrical or melting-plastic smell is different: stop the dishwasher, turn it off through its normal controls and arrange service rather than running another cycle.

Notice whether the smell is strongest when you first open the door, while the dishwasher drains or after the sink has been used. Those details help separate an interior-cleaning issue from a household plumbing or installation problem.

Clean the customer-accessible filter

Many modern dishwashers have a removable filter below the lower spray arm. Check the owner’s manual for the exact removal steps because designs differ. With the dishwasher off and cool, remove the lower rack, lift or turn only the filter parts intended for routine cleaning, and rinse them with warm water and a soft brush.

Do not use a screwdriver to pry components, reach into hidden openings or force a filter that will not release normally. Broken glass can collect below the rack, so wear suitable gloves and inspect carefully in good light.

A clean filter helps, but it is not a cure for every odor. If dirty water remains in the tub, the drain system or household plumbing still needs attention.

Wipe the places the wash spray misses

The bottom edge of the door, the gasket folds and the area around the detergent dispenser may not receive the same spray as the racks. Wipe those surfaces with a soft cloth and mild dish soap, then dry them. Avoid abrasive pads and strong chemical mixtures that can damage finishes or seals.

Pull the racks forward and remove labels, paper, bones, seeds and other debris that can lodge near the rails or screen. Check that tall utensils and pan handles are not preventing a spray arm from turning.

Standing water changes the diagnosis

A small amount of clean water in the sump can be normal on some models. Water covering the filter area, carrying food debris or returning after the dishwasher drains is not a maintenance problem to ignore. It may involve a blocked filter, restricted hose, disposal connection, check valve, pump or household drain.

Use the normal cancel-drain command once and listen. A pump that hums with little movement suggests a different problem than complete silence or a sink that gurgles. My guide to a dishwasher that will not drain explains the safest observations to make.

Check the sink and disposal connection

The dishwasher usually shares part of its drain path with the kitchen sink. If the sink drains slowly or the disposal holds food, odors can travel back toward the dishwasher. Run the disposal only according to its manufacturer’s instructions and with the correct water flow. Never put a hand into the disposal.

A drain hose should normally rise in a high loop beneath the counter or connect through an approved air gap, depending on the installation and local requirements. A hose lying low can allow sink water to migrate backward. Do not disconnect plumbing if you are unsure of the setup; a leak under the cabinet can cause more damage than the original odor.

Use detergent and cycles for the actual load

Too much detergent can leave a film that traps soil, while too little may not handle a heavy load. Use fresh automatic-dishwasher detergent and follow both the appliance and detergent directions. Hand dish soap does not belong in a dishwasher because it can create excessive suds and leaks.

Short or low-temperature cycles are useful for light soil, but using them for every greasy load can leave residue behind. Scrape large food pieces, load so spray reaches the dishes, and choose a cycle that matches the soil. For performance clues, see my guide to film and cloudy glassware in Jacksonville water.

Let moisture leave between loads

After the cycle ends and the dishes are cool, unload promptly and leave the door slightly open if the manufacturer allows it and the door is not a trip hazard. That helps the tub dry in humid homes from Jacksonville Beach and Atlantic Beach to Mandarin and Southside.

Do not drape a towel over the latch or defeat the door mechanism. If the door will not stay in a safe resting position, simply wipe the interior edge dry and keep the area around the machine ventilated.

When cleaning products help—and when they do not

A manufacturer-approved dishwasher cleaner can remove some mineral film and residue. Follow the label and the owner’s manual, and never combine cleaners, bleach, vinegar or ammonia. Mixing household chemicals can create dangerous fumes or damage stainless components and seals.

If odor returns immediately after a cleaning cycle, stop treating the symptom. Recurring standing water, poor wash results, leaks or drain noise call for diagnosis.

When to schedule dishwasher repair

Arrange service when water remains after drain, the smell resembles sewage, the pump sounds abnormal, the dishwasher leaks or the odor returns despite a clean filter and clear sink drain. FAQs

Can I pour bleach into a smelly dishwasher?

Do not assume bleach is safe for the tub, seals or stainless components. Use only products and procedures approved for the exact model, and never mix cleaning chemicals.

Why does the dishwasher smell worse after using the sink?

That pattern can point to a slow sink drain, disposal residue, a missing high loop or another drain-connection issue that lets odor or water move backward.

Should there be any water below the filter?

Some designs retain a small amount of clean water in the sump. Water covering the filter area, containing debris or returning after drain deserves closer inspection.
