A Practical Guide to Cleaning Dryer Moisture Sensors in Florida

Learn how to safely clean dryer moisture sensor bars, recognize coating-related drying problems and know when Jacksonville dryer service is needed.

By Mason Hedlund · Published OCT 16, 2026 · Jacksonville, FL

If an automatic dryer cycle ends while clothes are still damp—or runs long after the load is dry—the moisture sensors deserve a look. These small metal bars help the control estimate how wet the load is. A thin coating from fabric softener, dryer sheets or laundry products can make that reading less reliable.

In Florida, humid laundry rooms can make drying complaints harder to interpret because towels and clothes may begin the cycle with more ambient moisture. Cleaning the sensor bars is a simple maintenance step, but it is only one part of a complete airflow and heating diagnosis.

What the moisture sensors actually do

Most modern dryers use two metal strips inside the drum, usually near the lint-filter housing or front bulkhead. Damp fabric touches both strips and completes a very small sensing circuit. As the load dries, contact becomes less frequent and the control moves toward cooldown and shutoff.

The sensors do not measure humidity in the room like a weather station. They depend on wet fabric making contact. Very small loads, bulky items that tumble away from the bars and mixed loads with one heavy towel can all confuse an automatic cycle even when the dryer is working normally.

Find the bars with the dryer off

Turn the dryer off and unplug it before cleaning inside the drum. For a gas dryer, do not move the appliance or disturb the gas connection; cleaning the accessible bars does not require pulling the machine out.

Look for two narrow parallel metal strips near the lint-filter opening or just inside the front of the drum. Their location varies by brand and model, so check the owner's manual if they are not obvious. Do not remove panels or reach into openings to find them.

Clean gently, then dry the area

Use a soft cloth lightly dampened with warm water and a small amount of mild dish soap. Wipe the metal bars to remove residue, then wipe again with a clean damp cloth and dry them completely. The goal is to lift the film without scratching the metal or soaking nearby components.

Avoid steel wool, abrasive pads, solvent, bleach and heavy sprays. Do not pour cleaner into the drum or use a wet cloth around electrical openings. If residue will not come off with gentle cleaning, stop rather than grinding at the sensor surface.

Run a useful test load

After the bars are dry and the dryer is reconnected, test an automatic cycle with a normal mixed load—not one shirt and not an overfilled drum. Include several similar-weight items so damp fabric regularly contacts the sensor bars.

Compare that result with a timed cycle. If timed drying produces normal heat but automatic cycles end too early, the sensor system, load contact or control logic deserves closer attention. If both cycles take too long, airflow or heat is more likely than dirty sensor bars. My guide to a dryer that takes too long explains those broader clues.

Florida humidity can expose marginal drying

Jacksonville humidity does not usually cause a healthy dryer to fail, but it can add time to air-drying between loads and make a weak vent system more noticeable. Laundry rooms in garages, beach properties and tight closets may also run warmer and damper than the rest of the home.

Clean the lint screen after every load and make sure the exterior vent hood opens strongly while the dryer runs. Do not reach into a duct or dismantle the vent while the appliance is operating. If the dryer cabinet becomes unusually hot, clothes smell scorched or the vent has weak airflow, stop and arrange service. Those symptoms can be fire-safety concerns; review the dryer lint warning signs.

When cleaning is not the fix

Sensor bars are part of a system that can include wiring, thermistors, thermostats and the electronic control. A dryer may also stop early because heat is cycling incorrectly, the vent is restricted or the load is not tumbling properly. Replacing the sensors without testing can waste money.

How often should the bars be cleaned?

There is no universal schedule. Check them when automatic-cycle behavior changes or when you can see a waxy film. Homes that use dryer sheets or fabric softener frequently may need the bars wiped more often. Cleaning the sensors during routine lint-screen and drum care is reasonable as long as the dryer is unplugged and the method stays gentle.

FAQs

Can dryer sheets coat moisture sensors?

Yes. Conditioning ingredients can leave a film on the bars over time. The coating may reduce reliable contact with damp fabric.

Why does the dryer work on timed dry but not sensor dry?

That pattern can point toward dirty sensor bars, poor load contact, sensor wiring or the control. It is a useful clue, not a final diagnosis.

Will cleaning the sensors fix a no-heat dryer?

No. Moisture sensors help determine cycle length; they do not create heat. A no-heat complaint needs a separate electrical, gas, airflow and component diagnosis.
