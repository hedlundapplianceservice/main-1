Electric Stove Burner Not Heating? Check Whether One or All Failed

Hedlund Appliance Service · October 9, 2026 · 3 min read

One burner stops heating and you move dinner to the next one. That works for tonight, but the pattern is useful for diagnosis. A single affected burner differs from a range whose entire cooktop has lost heat while the clock still runs.

Start by identifying your cooking surface: exposed coils, radiant glass or induction. They do not use the same components or behave the same way.

Check the selected zone and controls

Confirm that you selected the intended burner and the correct portion of a multi-ring element. Look for lock settings or a displayed message. A radiant element cycling on and off can be normal temperature control rather than a failure.

If a control is damaged or stuck, do not force it. Record what happens when you use the normal operating procedure.

Induction needs suitable cookware

An induction zone can refuse to heat an unsuitable or poorly positioned pan. Check the manual’s cookware and size requirements. A magnetic-pan check can be a useful preliminary clue, but the manufacturer’s compatibility guidance is the better standard.

Try only a known suitable pan during normal operation, provided there are no electrical or damage warning signs. A blank zone with an error should be documented rather than repeatedly forced to run.

Coil and glass surfaces need different care

For removable coil elements, follow the exact owner procedure if inspection or reseating is permitted. Disconnect power and let everything cool first. Do not swap parts of different sizes or ratings on a guess.

Do not pry up a glass cooktop to inspect a radiant element. A cracked surface, sparking connection or scorched receptacle needs professional attention before further use.

If every burner is affected

A functioning clock does not prove the supply needed for heating is correct. Note whether the oven still heats and whether the problem followed electrical work or installation. A qualified technician or electrician may need to check the supply and appliance connections.

Do not repeatedly reset a circuit that trips. Do not move the range or modify its cord to work around the issue. Its installation and anti-tip protection also need to remain correct.

What needs testing

Depending on the design, an element, control, sensor, connection or induction component may be involved. A technician should establish where operation is interrupted before recommending a replacement.

Make your description count

Tell the technician which zone failed, which other functions still work and whether the surface or controls show damage. Include the model number and any error message. That narrows the visit far more effectively than saying the stove needs a new thermostat—and helps avoid replacing a heating component when the problem is actually cookware, controls or supply.

Before you schedule a visit

Take a photo of the model and serial label, and write down when the problem started. If the display shows an error, photograph the full code before clearing it. Mention recent installation work, a power outage, a move, or anything you already tried. Those details help narrow the diagnosis and identify the correct parts for your exact appliance.

Keep the owner’s manual handy. Controls, serviceable filters, installation requirements and normal sounds vary by model. A symptom can point toward several components; it cannot confirm which part needs replacement.
