Refrigerator Repair Guide

Refrigerator Making Noise? Separate Normal Sounds From Trouble

Hedlund Appliance Service · October 9, 2026 · 3 min read

You notice the refrigerator most when the house gets quiet. A soft hum can suddenly sound suspicious at midnight. Some refrigerator sounds are ordinary; a new scraping noise accompanied by warmer food deserves much more attention.

The useful question is not just, “Is it noisy?” It is, “What changed, when does it happen, and is it still cooling?”

Describe the sound before chasing a part

Record a short video from a safe distance. Capture the noise and mention whether it seems to come from the freezer, the bottom of the cabinet or the dispenser. Do not remove covers or reach around a running fan to locate it.

A recording does not replace testing, but it gives a technician something more specific than “a weird sound.” If the noise stops before the appointment, the video still helps.

Sounds that can be part of normal operation

Water entering the ice maker can briefly buzz. Ice dropping into a bin can knock. Refrigerant movement and defrosting can produce gurgling, hissing or dripping sounds. Modern variable-speed compressors may also run longer and sound different from an older refrigerator.

The important qualifiers are brief, familiar and consistent with normal cooling. A sound that becomes much louder or happens alongside poor temperature control should not be dismissed just because its name appears on a normal-noise list.

Check the easy rattles

Look for bottles touching each other, loose items on top of the cabinet or an accessible object vibrating against the appliance. Remove those items and listen again. Check the manual’s clearance and leveling requirements without moving a heavy unit alone.

A refrigerator touching a nearby cabinet can transmit vibration through the whole kitchen. That is different from an internal component grinding.

Scraping, squealing and repeated clicking

A scraping sound inside the freezer may involve a fan contacting ice or another obstruction. Squealing can come from a moving component. Repeated clicking with little cooling can point toward a starting or electrical problem, but it does not prove that the compressor itself has failed.

Avoid repeated resets as a diagnostic method. Note the temperature, preserve food safely and schedule testing if cooling is falling behind.

What to send when you call

  • A short recording of the sound.
  • The model number and approximate location of the noise.
  • Whether it changes during dispensing or when a door opens.
  • Actual compartment temperatures, if you have a thermometer.

If the refrigerator smells burnt, trips its circuit or has hot, damaged electrical connections, stop using it and arrange professional help. Otherwise, let the combination of noise and performance guide the next step. A harmless rattle should not earn you an unnecessary compressor replacement.

Before you schedule a visit

Take a photo of the model and serial label, and write down when the problem started. If the display shows an error, photograph the full code before clearing it. Mention recent installation work, a power outage, a move, or anything you already tried. Those details help narrow the diagnosis and identify the correct parts for your exact appliance.

Keep the owner’s manual handy. Controls, serviceable filters, installation requirements and normal sounds vary by model. A symptom can point toward several components; it cannot confirm which part needs replacement.
